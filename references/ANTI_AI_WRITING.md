# Anti-AI Writing

For the highest-priority writing rules, external-facing definition, thesis handling, resume safeguards, and full banned-pattern guidance, see [WRITING_POLICY.md](WRITING_POLICY.md).

## Remove empty confidence

Do not use trend language, jargon, or a framework to hide missing evidence. Delete phrases such as “AI wave,” “empower,” “paradigm shift,” “ecosystem,” “strategic high ground,” “deeply cultivate,” “significant potential,” or “it is worth noting” unless followed by a specific, decision-relevant fact.

Avoid vague hedges such as “may potentially have a certain impact.” Replace them with a condition, range, and decision consequence—or state that evidence is insufficient.

## Repair patterns

| Weak | Better |
| --- | --- |
| The market is huge. | The addressable segment is estimated at X–Y under stated adoption and price assumptions; Company A has no validated acquisition channel yet. |
| The model performed well. | On benchmark Z it improved metric M by N; production value remains unproven until error, latency, integration, and human-review costs are measured. |
| Recommend advancing. | Approve a 90-day pilot owned by Operations, capped at ¥X, with KPI Y; stop if error rate exceeds Z. |

## Editing checklist

- Can each adjective be replaced with a number, date, comparison, or condition?
- Does each paragraph make one claim and show why it matters?
- Are claims from the source, calculation, or author clearly separated?
- Would a skeptical executive understand what has not been proven?
- Can a templated contrast, AI identity disclaimer, unsupported hedge, or empty transition be replaced by a direct statement?
