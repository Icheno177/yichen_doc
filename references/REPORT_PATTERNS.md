# Report Patterns

Choose the shortest structure that lets the decision owner act. Do not force a framework into a report.

## Industry research

1. Decision question and answer.
2. Market boundary, customer/job, and time horizon.
3. Demand, supply, economics, and regulatory/technology facts.
4. Segment attractiveness and competitive dynamics.
5. Scenarios, risks, and recommended entry/no-entry actions.

PESTEL, Porter, RBV, SWOT, or value-chain analysis are optional. Include one only if it changes segment priority, entry mode, timing, or risk control.

## Due diligence

1. Investment/partnership decision and disqualifiers.
2. Company identity, ownership, legal/regulatory facts.
3. Product, customer, market, traction, and unit-economics evidence.
4. Management, governance, commercial, technical, and concentration risks.
5. Valuation/terms implications, conditions precedent, and decision.

Distinguish issuer claims from independently verified facts. A diligence report should make gaps and unresolved contradictions visible.

## Business case

1. Decision, alternatives, and status quo cost.
2. Scope, baseline, and target operating model.
3. Benefit mechanism and costs (implementation, operating, risk, opportunity cost).
4. ROI, TCO, payback, scenarios, and sensitivity.
5. Owner, plan, governance, KPI, and stop/go rule.

## AI research

1. Workflow and decision—not model novelty.
2. Baseline: volume, cycle time, quality, cost, risk, and human-review rate.
3. Capability evidence and evaluation relevance.
4. Integration, latency, error, data, security, adoption, and escalation constraints.
5. Agent/unit economics, experiment design, measured outcome, and scale decision.

Do not translate a benchmark result into enterprise ROI without a measured workflow bridge.

## Strategy memo

1. One-paragraph recommendation and confidence boundary.
2. Decision options and evidence that differentiates them.
3. Quantified trade-offs and risk register.
4. Action plan: owner, milestones, resource, KPI, stop/go.
