# Research Workflow

## Decision-first sequence

1. Define the decision owner, decision, deadline, scope, and error cost.
2. Turn the decision into 3–7 questions that can be proven, estimated, or falsified.
3. Build an evidence plan: claims needed, preferred evidence level, source owner, and known gaps.
4. Collect and log evidence before drafting a conclusion.
5. Normalize time period, geography, unit, currency, denominator, and definitions.
6. Calculate only quantities that change the decision.
7. Draft the executive answer, evidence, calculation, risk, and action chain.
8. Run a counterevidence and inference-bridge review.

## Research hygiene

- Prefer original filings, datasets, technical documentation, and methods over summaries.
- Capture publication date and the period the data describes; they are not interchangeable.
- Keep direct quotes short and distinguish them from your interpretation.
- If sources conflict, show the conflict, explain the likely definition or time difference, and do not average them without justification.
- Ask before accessing confidential internal systems or using non-public data.

## Minimum decision brief

Before extensive research, write:

| Field | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| Decision | The action or allocation under consideration |
| Owner | Person/team accountable for the choice |
| Deadline | When a decision is needed |
| Scope | Geography, product, customer, and time boundary |
| Success | KPI or decision criterion |
| Failure cost | Material downside if wrong |
| Unknowns | Facts that could reverse the conclusion |

## Falsification pass

For each pivotal judgment, ask:

1. What evidence would make this false?
2. What alternative explanation fits the same facts?
3. Is a claim being carried by a proxy rather than direct evidence?
4. Does the recommendation still hold in a Bear case?
5. Does an owner have authority, time, budget, and a measurable stop/go trigger?
